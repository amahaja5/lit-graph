For researchers who cite carefully

Find the papers. Keep the references. Check the claims.

Groundwork maps the literature around a paper, puts a manuscript's bibliography into your Zotero library, and checks whether each cited source actually supports the sentence that cites it, showing the quote and the page.

Open Groundwork See how it works

Exploring papers is open to everyone. Importing references and checking citations need a Zotero sign-in from an approved account.

How it works

  1. 1

    Explore papers

    Start from a DOI, arXiv, PubMed or NBER identifier and see the paper's citation graph: the work it builds on and the work that cites it, ranked and laid out by year. Snowball outward one paper at a time.

    Collect papers in a review cart, export BibTeX, or save the cart to a Zotero collection as a reading list.

  2. 2

    Import references

    Upload a manuscript PDF and Groundwork extracts its bibliography with GROBID, matches each entry on Crossref (and arXiv papers on DataCite), and saves clear matches to a new Zotero collection. Anything uncertain waits for your review.

    Then let Zotero's Find Full Text fetch the PDFs.

  3. 3

    Check citations

    Groundwork finds every cited passage in the manuscript and reads the cited PDF from your Zotero library. It reports whether the source supports, partly supports or contradicts the claim, or doesn't say, with the exact quote and page so you can verify it yourself.

Built around your Zotero library

Your references and PDFs stay in Zotero, where you already keep them. Groundwork signs in with Zotero, writes only to the collections it creates, reuses items you already have (matched by DOI), and reads the PDFs you've attached, including paywalled ones you have access to.

What gets sent where

Manuscript PDFs go to a GROBID service for reference extraction; text inputs and citation checks go to Anthropic's Claude. Reference strings and DOIs are looked up on Crossref and DataCite. Groundwork doesn't store your manuscript; unfinished work is lost when you close the page.

Questions

Do I need Zotero?

Exploring papers works without it. Importing references and checking citations use your Zotero library, so you'll need a free Zotero account with file syncing to zotero.org for your PDFs.

What does "supported" mean?

That the retrieved passages of the cited source back the specific claim, including its numbers, qualifiers and scope. It is not a judgement that the source is correct. If the full text isn't available, Groundwork says so rather than guessing.

How large can a manuscript be?

Up to 3 MB and 80 references per import. Scanned PDFs need OCR first; split longer works into sections with their bibliographies.

Which papers can it find?

Exploring uses the Semantic Scholar Graph API. Reference matching uses Crossref, plus DataCite for arXiv preprints; papers outside both stay unresolved for you to review or add by hand.
